Group Planning Exercises

The GTO sets a group down in a place with named resources and a clock running, several problems arrive at once, and you write a plan before the group argues its way to a shared one. Prioritising under that pressure is the thing being measured. Here are 54 of them to practise on, in English and Urdu.

The exercises

Read the narrative, then write your own plan before opening anything else: list the problems in the order you would deal with them, say who goes where and with what, and give your reason for that order. Do not add resources the narrative does not mention.

Every one of these is original. The scenarios, distances, resources and plans were written for this platform. Nothing here is copied from ISSB materials, prep books or past papers — which is also why this site carries no scanned sheets or question banks.

1

Flood warning above the village

گاؤں کے اوپر سیلاب کی اطلاع

medium

You are eight college students camping on the sandy bank of a river, two kilometres downstream of the village of Kot Basra. It is 7 p.m. and the light is going. A forest guard rides up and tells you that heavy rain in the hills has burst a small dam eight hours upstream; the water will reach this stretch by about midnight. The village has roughly four hundred people and no telephone line. The only road out of the village runs north and crosses a wooden causeway that the guard says will go under first, probably before 10 p.m. Two of your group are strong swimmers. You have one tractor-trolley borrowed from a farmer, a bicycle, four torches, a first-aid box and your own tents. In the village there are about thirty elderly people, and a primary school with a flat concrete roof stands on high ground at the eastern edge. One of your own group, Adil, went up the hill an hour ago to photograph the sunset and has not come back.

Read in Urdu

آپ آٹھ کالج کے طالب علم ایک دریا کے ریتلے کنارے پر خیمہ زن ہیں، جو گاؤں کوٹ بصرہ سے دو کلومیٹر نیچے کی جانب ہے۔ شام کے سات بجے ہیں اور روشنی کم ہو رہی ہے۔ محکمہ جنگلات کا ایک گارڈ آ کر بتاتا ہے کہ پہاڑوں میں شدید بارش سے آٹھ گھنٹے کے فاصلے پر ایک چھوٹا بند ٹوٹ گیا ہے؛ پانی تقریباً آدھی رات تک یہاں پہنچ جائے گا۔ گاؤں میں تقریباً چار سو افراد ہیں اور کوئی ٹیلی فون لائن نہیں۔ گاؤں سے نکلنے کا واحد راستہ شمال کی طرف جاتا ہے اور ایک لکڑی کے پُل سے گزرتا ہے جو گارڈ کے مطابق سب سے پہلے ڈوبے گا، غالباً رات دس بجے سے پہلے۔ آپ کے گروپ میں دو اچھے تیراک ہیں۔ آپ کے پاس ایک کسان سے لی ہوئی ٹریکٹر ٹرالی، ایک سائیکل، چار ٹارچیں، ابتدائی طبی امداد کا بکس اور اپنے خیمے ہیں۔ گاؤں میں تقریباً تیس بزرگ افراد ہیں، اور مشرقی کنارے پر بلند زمین پر ایک پرائمری اسکول ہے جس کی چھت پختہ اور ہموار ہے۔ آپ کے اپنے گروپ کا ایک ساتھی عادل ایک گھنٹہ پہلے غروبِ آفتاب کی تصویر لینے پہاڑی پر گیا تھا اور واپس نہیں آیا۔

Problems in this narrative (4)
  1. Four hundred villagers are unwarned and have under five hours.
  2. The causeway — the only road out — closes about two hours before the water arrives.
  3. Thirty elderly villagers cannot walk out in the time available.
  4. Adil is missing on the hill in failing light.
One defensible plan
  1. Send two people on the tractor-trolley to the village at once. A warning is the only thing that turns five hours into five hours of work for four hundred people instead of for eight; every minute it is delayed is lost by everybody.
  2. Send one person on the bicycle north across the causeway while it is still open, to reach the police post and report the breach. This is the only message that can bring help the group cannot provide itself, and its window shuts first.
  3. Use the tractor-trolley in repeated runs to move the elderly and the sick to the school roof on the high ground, not north up the road. The high ground is minutes away and the road is hours, and the trolley cannot clear four hundred people in any case.
  4. Send the two strongest walkers up the hill with two torches to find Adil, on a fixed turnaround time. One life is not worth more than four hundred, but two people can be spared and the search must not become a second emergency.
  5. Everyone else walks the able-bodied villagers to the school and the eastern high ground, taking the tents and the first-aid box. After midnight the problem stops being the water and becomes cold, wet people with nowhere to sit.

This is one workable order, not the only one. A plan that reaches the same ends in a different order, with reasons given, is equally good — which is the whole point of the exercise.

2

Bus down on the mountain road

پہاڑی سڑک پر بس خراب

medium

Your group of seven is travelling by coach on a single-track mountain road when the driver stops: a rockfall has put a boulder and a spill of loose stone across the road, and the coach cannot pass. It is 4 p.m. in December and it will be dark by half past five. There are twenty-six passengers, including four children and an elderly woman who is diabetic and tells you her insulin went ahead this morning in a relative's car. The nearest village is six kilometres back downhill; the nearest town with a hospital is thirty-one kilometres ahead. There is no mobile signal here, but the driver says there is signal at the shrine on the ridge, about two kilometres back and three hundred metres up. The coach carries a toolkit, a spare tyre, two blankets and a quarter tank of diesel. Behind you a truck has stopped, and its driver will help but will not leave his cargo.

Read in Urdu

آپ کا سات افراد کا گروپ ایک تنگ پہاڑی سڑک پر کوچ میں سفر کر رہا ہے کہ ڈرائیور رُک جاتا ہے: تودہ گرنے سے ایک بڑا پتھر اور ڈھیلے پتھروں کا ڈھیر سڑک پر آ گیا ہے اور کوچ گزر نہیں سکتی۔ دسمبر کی شام کے چار بج رہے ہیں اور ساڑھے پانچ بجے اندھیرا ہو جائے گا۔ بس میں چھبیس مسافر ہیں، جن میں چار بچے اور ایک بزرگ خاتون شامل ہیں جو ذیابیطس کی مریضہ ہیں اور بتاتی ہیں کہ اُن کی انسولین آج صبح ایک رشتہ دار کی گاڑی میں آگے چلی گئی ہے۔ قریب ترین گاؤں چھ کلومیٹر واپس نیچے کی جانب ہے؛ ہسپتال والا قریب ترین شہر اکتیس کلومیٹر آگے ہے۔ یہاں موبائل سگنل نہیں، لیکن ڈرائیور کہتا ہے کہ پہاڑی کی چوٹی پر واقع مزار کے پاس سگنل آتا ہے، جو تقریباً دو کلومیٹر پیچھے اور تین سو میٹر بلندی پر ہے۔ کوچ میں اوزاروں کا بکس، ایک اضافی ٹائر، دو کمبل اور چوتھائی ٹینک ڈیزل ہے۔ آپ کے پیچھے ایک ٹرک رُکا ہے جس کا ڈرائیور مدد پر تیار ہے مگر اپنا سامان چھوڑنے کو تیار نہیں۔

Problems in this narrative (4)
  1. A diabetic passenger is separated from her insulin with no clinic within thirty-one kilometres.
  2. Twenty-six people, four of them children, face a December night at altitude with two blankets.
  3. The road is blocked and nobody outside knows it.
  4. Darkness in ninety minutes limits everything that involves walking.
One defensible plan
  1. Send two of the group to the shrine at once, with directions from the driver, to call the police post, the hospital ahead and the relative carrying the insulin. Two kilometres uphill uses most of the remaining daylight, so this cannot be the second decision.
  2. While they walk, ask the woman when she last took insulin, and keep her warm, still and in company. Most of what can go wrong in the next four hours is made worse by cold and panic, and both are treatable with the blankets and the engine run in bursts on the quarter tank.
  3. Put the fit men and the truck driver on clearing the loose stone by hand with the toolkit, starting at the downhill edge. Even a single-vehicle gap gets one car through, and it can be done in the light that is left.
  4. Keep all twenty-six passengers at the coach rather than spread along the road. A cold, dark, single-track road with a rockfall above it is the worst place to stand, and a group in one place is a group that can be counted.
  5. If the boulder has not moved by dark, stop work and plan the night: children and the elderly in the middle seats with the blankets, engine run ten minutes each hour, one person on watch at each end with a torch for any vehicle that comes.

This is one workable order, not the only one. A plan that reaches the same ends in a different order, with reasons given, is equally good — which is the whole point of the exercise.

Fire in the cloth market

کپڑا بازار میں آگ

hard

You are six friends walking through a covered cloth market at 8 p.m. when smoke begins pushing out of a shuttered shop in the middle row. The market is a warren of narrow lanes with fabric stacked to the ceiling, and perhaps two hundred shoppers and shopkeepers are inside. There are two ways out: the main gate to the south, thirty metres away and already crowding, and a service gate to the north that is usually padlocked. The watchman with the keys is somewhere in the market. A hydrant stands outside the main gate but you have no hose. Above the burning row are two floors of small flats, and you can hear a child crying from one of them. Two shopkeepers are dragging bolts of cloth out into the lane and narrowing it. Your group has three phones with signal, a bottle of water each, and one of you holds a first-aid certificate.

Read in Urdu

آپ چھ دوست رات آٹھ بجے ایک چھت والے کپڑا بازار میں چل رہے ہیں کہ درمیانی قطار کی ایک بند دکان سے دھواں نکلنے لگتا ہے۔ بازار تنگ گلیوں کا جال ہے جہاں کپڑا چھت تک ڈھیر ہے، اور اندر تقریباً دو سو خریدار اور دکاندار موجود ہیں۔ باہر نکلنے کے دو راستے ہیں: جنوب میں مرکزی دروازہ، جو تیس میٹر دور ہے اور وہاں رش بڑھ رہا ہے، اور شمال میں سروس گیٹ جو عموماً تالا بند رہتا ہے۔ چابیاں رکھنے والا چوکیدار بازار میں کہیں موجود ہے۔ مرکزی دروازے کے باہر ایک ہائیڈرنٹ ہے مگر آپ کے پاس پائپ نہیں۔ جلتی ہوئی قطار کے اوپر چھوٹے فلیٹوں کی دو منزلیں ہیں، اور اُن میں سے ایک سے کسی بچے کے رونے کی آواز آ رہی ہے۔ دو دکاندار اپنی دکانوں سے کپڑے کے تھان کھینچ کر گلی میں ڈال رہے ہیں جس سے گلی مزید تنگ ہو رہی ہے۔ آپ کے گروپ کے پاس سگنل والے تین فون، ایک ایک پانی کی بوتل ہے، اور آپ میں سے ایک کے پاس ابتدائی طبی امداد کا سرٹیفکیٹ ہے۔

Problems in this narrative (4)
  1. Two hundred people are heading for one usable exit through fabric-lined lanes.
  2. A child is above the fire on the first or second floor.
  3. The north service gate is locked and the watchman is unlocated.
  4. Shopkeepers saving stock are narrowing the escape route.
One defensible plan
  1. One person calls Rescue 1122 and the fire brigade from outside the main gate and stays there to meet and direct them. Someone who can say which row and which lane saves the brigade several minutes, and nothing else the group does will put this fire out.
  2. Two people find the watchman or break the north padlock. Two hundred people through one gate is the thing most likely to kill someone tonight, and a second exit halves the crush.
  3. Two people work the lanes, moving shoppers towards the exits and stopping the shopkeepers stacking cloth. Say it as an instruction, not a request — a blocked lane is a trapped lane.
  4. Two people go for the child by the outside stair if there is one, and only while that stair is clear of smoke. Send word to the brigade that there are flats above with a child in one, so that if you must turn back the information has already arrived.
  5. Set the first-aider up at the hydrant outside the main gate as the collecting point and start counting people out. Smoke casualties walk out and then collapse; a known place to take them is worth more than another pair of hands in the lane.

This is one workable order, not the only one. A plan that reaches the same ends in a different order, with reasons given, is equally good — which is the whole point of the exercise.

Landslide across the railway line

ریلوے لائن پر لینڈ سلائیڈنگ

medium

Your group of nine is walking a hill track at 3 p.m. when a slope gives way below you and slides onto the railway line in the valley, burying about forty metres of track. From where you stand the line curves out of a tunnel eight hundred metres to the east; a board at the track says the afternoon passenger train passes at 4.10 p.m. A gangman's hut beside the line holds a red flag and a hand lamp. A farmer tells you his brother's tea stall at the level crossing, three kilometres west, has a telephone. Two of your group are quick and wearing running shoes; one has a sprained ankle from the morning. The nearest station is eleven kilometres east, beyond the tunnel. It is bright now but cloud is building, and rain would bring more of the slope down. There is no mobile signal in the valley.

Read in Urdu

آپ کا نو افراد کا گروپ سہ پہر تین بجے ایک پہاڑی پگڈنڈی پر چل رہا ہے کہ آپ کے نیچے ڈھلوان بیٹھ جاتی ہے اور وادی میں ریلوے لائن پر آ گرتی ہے، جس سے تقریباً چالیس میٹر پٹری دب جاتی ہے۔ جہاں آپ کھڑے ہیں وہاں سے لائن مشرق میں آٹھ سو میٹر دور ایک سرنگ سے نکلتی دکھائی دیتی ہے؛ پٹری کے پاس لگے بورڈ کے مطابق سہ پہر کی مسافر ٹرین چار بج کر دس منٹ پر گزرتی ہے۔ لائن کے ساتھ گینگ مین کی جھونپڑی میں سرخ جھنڈی اور ہاتھ والی لالٹین موجود ہے۔ ایک کسان بتاتا ہے کہ تین کلومیٹر مغرب میں پھاٹک پر اُس کے بھائی کے چائے کے کھوکھے میں ٹیلی فون ہے۔ آپ کے گروپ میں دو افراد تیز ہیں اور دوڑنے والے جوتے پہنے ہوئے ہیں؛ ایک کے ٹخنے میں صبح سے موچ ہے۔ قریب ترین اسٹیشن گیارہ کلومیٹر مشرق میں، سرنگ کے پار ہے۔ اس وقت دھوپ ہے مگر بادل بن رہے ہیں، اور بارش سے مزید ڈھلوان گر سکتی ہے۔ وادی میں موبائل سگنل نہیں ہے۔

Problems in this narrative (4)
  1. A passenger train is due in about seventy minutes and will meet the slide at speed out of a blind tunnel.
  2. Nobody at either station knows the line is blocked.
  3. More of the slope may come down, on the line and on anyone standing on it.
  4. One of your own group cannot move quickly.
One defensible plan
  1. Send the two fastest runners east with the red flag and the hand lamp, to a point well beyond the tunnel mouth rather than at the slide. A driver leaving a tunnel needs warning far enough back to stop; a flag at the obstruction is a flag he sees too late.
  2. Send two more west to the tea stall to telephone the station and railway control. A stopped train is one train; a message to control stops everything on the section and starts the clearance.
  3. Keep everyone else off the slide and off the line, on the side away from the slope. The second fall is the one that catches the people who came to look at the first.
  4. Leave the sprained ankle at the gangman hut as a fixed point, to flag down and brief anyone who arrives. Someone who cannot run can still be the person who knows what happened and where everyone went.
  5. Once the train is stopped and control is informed, note the length of the slide and whether the slope is still moving, and give both facts to the first railway staff who arrive. The clearance plan starts there.

This is one workable order, not the only one. A plan that reaches the same ends in a different order, with reasons given, is equally good — which is the whole point of the exercise.

Canal breach above the fields

کھیتوں کے اوپر نہر میں شگاف

medium

You are five agriculture students staying at a farm when the caretaker wakes you at 5 a.m.: the main canal bank has breached about a kilometre upstream and water is spreading across the fields towards the farm buildings. The breach is roughly three metres wide and widening slowly. Below it lie forty acres of standing wheat, a tube-well room holding the farm's electric motor and switchgear, a shed with eleven tethered buffaloes, and the farmhouse where the caretaker's family of six is asleep. The canal patwari lives at the head regulator four kilometres up the bank and can order the flow cut; there is no phone at the farm. You have a tractor with a front blade, about two hundred empty fertiliser sacks, shovels, and eight farm workers who arrive at six. Sunrise is at a quarter past six. The farm motorcycle has fuel for perhaps ten kilometres.

Read in Urdu

آپ پانچ زرعی طلبہ ایک فارم پر ٹھہرے ہوئے ہیں کہ نگران صبح پانچ بجے آپ کو جگاتا ہے: تقریباً ایک کلومیٹر اوپر مرکزی نہر کا کنارہ ٹوٹ گیا ہے اور پانی کھیتوں سے ہوتا ہوا فارم کی عمارتوں کی طرف پھیل رہا ہے۔ شگاف تقریباً تین میٹر چوڑا ہے اور آہستہ آہستہ بڑھ رہا ہے۔ اس کے نیچے چالیس ایکڑ کھڑی گندم، ایک ٹیوب ویل کا کمرہ جس میں فارم کی بجلی کی موٹر اور سوئچ گیئر ہے، ایک باڑہ جس میں گیارہ بھینسیں بندھی ہیں، اور فارم ہاؤس ہے جہاں نگران کا چھ افراد پر مشتمل خاندان سو رہا ہے۔ نہر کا پٹواری چار کلومیٹر اوپر ہیڈ ریگولیٹر پر رہتا ہے اور پانی بند کرانے کا حکم دے سکتا ہے؛ فارم پر کوئی فون نہیں۔ آپ کے پاس آگے بلیڈ والا ٹریکٹر، تقریباً دو سو خالی کھاد کی بوریاں، بیلچے، اور آٹھ فارم ورکرز ہیں جو چھ بجے پہنچتے ہیں۔ سورج سوا چھ بجے طلوع ہوگا۔ فارم کی موٹر سائیکل میں تقریباً دس کلومیٹر کا ایندھن ہے۔

Problems in this narrative (4)
  1. The breach will keep widening until the canal flow is cut at the regulator.
  2. A sleeping family of six is in the path of the water.
  3. Eleven tethered buffaloes cannot free themselves.
  4. Live electrical switchgear is about to be flooded.
One defensible plan
  1. Wake the family and move them, with documents and shoes, to the roof or the high yard. They are the only irreplaceable thing on the farm and it costs two minutes.
  2. Send one student on the motorcycle to the head regulator to get the flow cut. Ten kilometres of fuel covers the four there and back, and until the flow stops, every sack the rest of you fill is being poured into a rising river.
  3. Throw the main switch at the tube-well room before the water reaches the switchgear. A flooded live board turns a wet field into a place nobody can work in.
  4. Untie the buffaloes and drive them to the high ground by the road. Tethered animals drown where they stand and loose animals mostly save themselves; it takes two people ten minutes.
  5. Put everyone else, and the eight workers as they arrive, on the tractor and the sacks — narrowing the breach from both ends rather than damming the middle, and only once the flow is falling. The wheat is last on the list and the first thing everybody will want to start with.

This is one workable order, not the only one. A plan that reaches the same ends in a different order, with reasons given, is equally good — which is the whole point of the exercise.

Missing boy on the sand dunes

ریت کے ٹیلوں میں گم شدہ لڑکا

medium

Your group of ten is at a desert camp near Khairpur. At 5 p.m. a herder arrives: his nine-year-old son took the goats out at noon and has not come back, and the goats have returned without him. The boy was last seen heading north-east towards a belt of high dunes about four kilometres away. Sunset is at 6.20 p.m. and the temperature falls sharply after dark; the boy has no water. The camp has two jeeps, one with a full tank and one with a quarter, six torches, a whistle, four litres of drinking water per person and a vehicle horn. There is mobile signal at the camp and none in the dunes. The nearest police post is twenty-two kilometres by road. A dust haze is cutting visibility, and the herder says his own tracks and the goats' tracks cross the whole area. Four of your group have never walked in dunes.

Problems in this narrative (4)
  1. Nine people are on a leaking boat that will be submerged when the tide returns at eight.
  2. The motorboat carries four at a time and the mud cannot be walked.
  3. Two of the nine cannot swim and one is elderly.
  4. There is no mobile signal and the nearest settlement is five kilometres away.
One defensible plan
  1. Start ferrying people off now, while the falling tide still leaves water in the channel. Waiting to plan costs the one hour in which a boat can still reach them; after that the motorboat cannot cross the mud either.
  2. Take the elderly woman and the two non-swimmers in the first run, with both life jackets. They are the people for whom a later run or a swim is not an option, and the first trip is the only certain one.
  3. Send the satellite message as soon as the problem is understood: position, nine people, boat sinking, tide rising at eight. It is one-way and slow, so it has to go early and has to include the deadline.
  4. Rig the rope from the bank to the boat on the first run as a hand line. If the motor fails or the water drops too far, a fixed line across soft mud is the difference between a crossing and nobody moving at all.
  5. Leave the rice. Every sack carried is a person not carried, and the crew will want to save their cargo — say plainly that the boat is lost at eight o'clock and only the people have to be off it before then.

This is one workable order, not the only one. A plan that reaches the same ends in a different order, with reasons given, is equally good — which is the whole point of the exercise.

Problems in this narrative (4)
  1. Two students are missing at a highway stop fifty kilometres back, at night.
  2. Forty students and the bus cannot simply be parked on a highway.
  3. One missing phone is off and the other is unanswered.
  4. Two teachers want to delay reporting until morning.
One defensible plan
  1. Phone the hotel at the stop from the receipt number straight away and ask the staff to look for them and to hold them there. The only people who can search that stop in the next five minutes are already standing in it.
  2. Report to the police station in this town now, with names, descriptions, the stop and the time. Waiting until morning is the one decision here that cannot be undone, and a report that turns out to be unnecessary costs nothing but an apology.
  3. Send one teacher and two student leaders back by taxi or hired car rather than turning the bus. It gets searchers to the stop in an hour without leaving forty students on a highway, which is the trade the bus cannot make.
  4. Take the rest of the party on to the hostel and account for every remaining student by name on arrival. A tour that loses two students at nine o'clock should not discover a third at midnight.

GTO outdoor tasks

These are physical and group-based. They cannot be practised in an app, and any product that claims otherwise is selling you something. Use them to understand the principle each task tests and the mistake that most often costs marks.

Progressive Group Task (PGT)

Principle: Cross obstacles as a team using given helping material; observe rules such as colour bars and out-of-bounds markings.

Common mistake: Ignoring rules to finish faster; dominating instead of cooperating.

Half Group Task (HGT)

Principle: The same as PGT with a smaller group — your individual contribution is visible.

Common mistake: Going quiet; not offering workable ideas.

Individual Obstacles

Principle: Attempt as many of the ten obstacles as possible in the time; points scale with difficulty.

Common mistake: Poor time management; skipping the high-value obstacles.

Command Task

Principle: As commander, solve an obstacle using subordinates; give clear, confident briefings.

Common mistake: Vague orders; not involving the subordinates you were given.

Final Group Task

Principle: A last cooperative obstacle — maintain team spirit and stamina.

Common mistake: Fatigue-driven irritability; loss of coordination.

Four-week fitness plan

Build up gradually rather than peaking in week one.

  • Daily: 20–30 minutes running, building from 2 km to 5 km.
  • Bodyweight: push-ups, sit-ups, chin-ups — three sets, progressive reps.
  • Agility: shuttle runs, and rope climb practice where available.
  • Rest one day a week; sleep seven to eight hours; hydrate well.
